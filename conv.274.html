    Expected and Provided Terms#

    Purpose

    This convention defines Expected and Provided Terms. It is a method for evaluating a text by what it actually explains: the text is analyzed through its terms, each of which is either an expected term or a provided term. All other definitions build on this distinction.

    Definitions#

    Argument (conv. 274)#

    ~ is an elaboration whose statements are connected through reasoning to support its claim.

    See also

    • Claim (conv. 274)

    • Elaboration (conv. 274)

    • Enumeration of features (conv. 274)

    • Statement (conv. 274)

    Author’s privilege (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a principle of trusting the author in their judgement about the terms they define. ~ does not apply to expected terms.

    See also

    • Term (conv. 274)

    Balancing (conv. 274)#

    ~ is organizing text in such a way that those parts that are recognized to be on the same level (structurally or semantically) must carry a comparable degree of detail.

    See also

    • Claim (conv. 274)

    • Elaboration (conv. 274)

    • Expected term (conv. 274)

    • Text (conv. 274)

    Broader substitute (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a broader term used where its narrower term already exists in the text: a term more general in meaning replaces a more specific term.

    See also

    • Broader term (conv. 274)

    • Derived term (conv. 274)

    • Narrower term (conv. 274)

    • Term variant (conv. 274)

    • Text (conv. 274)

    Broader term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a term whose meaning fully includes the meaning of another term.

    See also

    • Broader substitute (conv. 274)

    • Narrower term (conv. 274)

    Claim (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a statement made of expected terms that the author chooses to explain.

    See also

    • Argument (conv. 274)

    • Balancing (conv. 274)

    • Elaboration (conv. 274)

    • Provided term (conv. 274)

    • Statement (conv. 274)

    Dangling reference (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a reference to something the text has never introduced.

    See also

    • Definition by reference (conv. 274)

    • Reference (conv. 274)

    Declared list (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a list introduced by an expected term that states the list is complete. By this convention, ~ defines the term it follows based on author’s privilege.

    See also

    • Author’s privilege (conv. 274)

    • Definition (conv. 274)

    • Illustration (conv. 274)

    • Unqualified list (conv. 274)

    Defining category (conv. 274)#

    ~ is the more general term into which a definition places the term it defines.

    See also

    • Definition (conv. 274)

    • Distinguishing feature (conv. 274)

    • Inflated category (conv. 274)

    Definition (conv. 274)#

    ~ relates a term to other terms so that the reader can tell what the term is. In a text, ~ takes one of four forms:

    1. a defining category with a distinguishing feature

    2. an elaboration, which defines a claim

    3. a definition by reference

    4. a declared list

    A partial definition is a weak form that may be used in contexts where a general idea of what the term represents would suffice for the purpose of the text.

    Important

    An illustration and an unqualified list are not definitions.

    See also

    • Claim (conv. 274)

    • Declared list (conv. 274)

    • Defining category (conv. 274)

    • Definition by reference (conv. 274)

    • Distinguishing feature (conv. 274)

    • Elaboration (conv. 274)

    • Partial definition (conv. 274)

    • Provided term (conv. 274)

    • Text (conv. 274)

    Definition by reference (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a definition that sends the reader to a definition elsewhere, either explicitly, pointing to a specific section, or implicitly, by using a broader reference which can be resolved unambiguously. ~ is valid only if its target is within the reader’s reach, is specific, and is a definition whose terms the reader already has at the point of the reference.

    See also

    • Broader substitute (conv. 274)

    • Dangling reference (conv. 274)

    • Definition (conv. 274)

    • Promised and alien reference (conv. 274)

    • Promised and vague reference (conv. 274)

    • Reference (conv. 274)

    Derived term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is an expected term that enters the verified term catalog as a part of another term without being introduced by itself.

    See also

    • Broader substitute (conv. 274)

    • Verified term catalog (conv. 274)

    Distinguishing feature (conv. 274)#

    ~ is the part of a definition that separates the term being defined from the other terms of its defining category within the text. According to the author’s privilege, ~ is sufficient if the term cannot be confused with another term in the text.

    See also

    • Defining category (conv. 274)

    • Definition (conv. 274)

    Elaboration (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a set of connected statements that support a claim.

    See also

    • Argument (conv. 274)

    • Claim (conv. 274)

    • Definition (conv. 274)

    • Enumeration of features (conv. 274)

    • Statement (conv. 274)

    Enumeration of features (conv. 274)#

    ~ is an elaboration that supports its claim by listing features of what the claim is about. The strength of the support depends on what the list contains.

    Important

    ~ is a list. Without an expected term introducing it, ~ is also an unqualified list.

    See also

    • Argument (conv. 274)

    • Author’s privilege (conv. 274)

    • Elaboration (conv. 274)

    • Unqualified list (conv. 274)

    Expected term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a term the text uses without explanation because the reader is expected to know it.

    See also

    • Balancing (conv. 274)

    • Insider term (conv. 274)

    • Misused expected term (conv. 274)

    • Provided term (conv. 274)

    • Term (conv. 274)

    • Term association (conv. 274)

    Hedge (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a word or phrase that softens the commitment of a statement or a relation, such as usually, should, primarily, or for as in “model for cybersecurity”.

    Important

    A word such as “usually” that introduces the features of a term marks a partial definition, whether or not it is also a ~.

    See also

    • Partial definition (conv. 274)

    • Relation (conv. 274)

    Illustration (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a list introduced by an expected term to present examples of a term. ~ does not define the term.

    See also

    • Declared list (conv. 274)

    • Definition (conv. 274)

    • Unqualified list (conv. 274)

    Incidental term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a provided term the text could do without: removing it loses nothing, however often it is used.

    See also

    • Provided term (conv. 274)

    • Redundant term (conv. 274)

    • Term (conv. 274)

    Inflated category (conv. 274)#

    ~ is an oversized defining category, such as “paradigm” for a model.

    See also

    • Defining category (conv. 274)

    Insider term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a pseudo-term whose definition is settled by the shared context of the author’s group.

    See also

    • Expected term (conv. 274)

    • Provided term (conv. 274)

    • Pseudo-term (conv. 274)

    • Term (conv. 274)

    • Unqualified list (conv. 274)

    Misused expected term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is an expected term used in a meaning that contradicts the meaning the reader already knows, such as “model” used for “approach”.

    Being an expected term, ~ is not within author’s privilege.

    See also

    • Author’s privilege (conv. 274)

    • Expected term (conv. 274)

    • Term variant (conv. 274)

    Narrower term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a term whose meaning is fully included in the meaning of its broader term.

    See also

    • Broader substitute (conv. 274)

    • Broader term (conv. 274)

    Partial definition (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a definition that states the common or essential features of a term and includes a hedge that effectively reduces the number of situations where this definition applies. ~ is weaker than the other forms of definition, but gives the reader enough to understand what the term is about according to the author’s privilege.

    See also

    • Author’s privilege (conv. 274)

    • Definition (conv. 274)

    • Hedge (conv. 274)

    Promised and alien reference (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a definition by reference whose target is a definition built on terms the reader does not have at the point of the reference.

    See also

    • Definition by reference (conv. 274)

    • Promised and vague reference (conv. 274)

    Promised and vague reference (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a definition by reference whose target is not a definition in any of its forms, or does not exist.

    See also

    • Definition by reference (conv. 274)

    • Promised and alien reference (conv. 274)

    Provided term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a term the author defines in the text via the recognized definition forms.

    Note

    A claim is a ~.

    See also

    • Claim (conv. 274)

    • Definition (conv. 274)

    • Expected term (conv. 274)

    • Term (conv. 274)

    • Term association (conv. 274)

    Pseudo-term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a word, phrase, or statement used as a term while its definition is neither provided by the author nor known to the reader.

    See also

    • Expected term (conv. 274)

    • Insider term (conv. 274)

    • Provided term (conv. 274)

    • Term (conv. 274)

    Redundant term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a term introduced but not used as introduced, such as an abbreviation that does not replace the full form in the rest of the text.

    See also

    • Incidental term (conv. 274)

    • Term variant (conv. 274)

    Reference (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a word or phrase that points to a term or a definition elsewhere in the text without fully qualifying it.

    See also

    • Term (conv. 274)

    • Text (conv. 274)

    Relation (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a connection between two terms, or between a term and a statement, named by an expected term.

    Important

    A ~ serves the verified term catalog: analyzing ~s reveals terms that are missing or misused. The name of a ~ can itself be a hedge or a misused expected term.

    See also

    • Hedge (conv. 274)

    • Statement (conv. 274)

    • Verified term catalog (conv. 274)

    Statement (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a fragment of text, a sentence or clause, considered as the smallest context when retrieving unverified terms for the unabridged term register.

    See also

    • Claim (conv. 274)

    • Unabridged term register (conv. 274)

    • Unverified term (conv. 274)

    Term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a word, phrase, or statement associated with a definition provided by the author or known to the reader.

    Reasoning

    ~s are the best means available for explaining something. Without them, an explanation becomes casual, and different readers take away different ideas. This convention uses ~ more broadly than a dictionary does: it requires no branch of study and no concept, and it is not limited to nouns.

    See also

    • Claim (conv. 274)

    • Definition (conv. 274)

    • Expected term (conv. 274)

    • Insider term (conv. 274)

    • Provided term (conv. 274)

    • Verified term catalog (conv. 274)

    Term association (conv. 274)#

    ~ is the label a term receives in the verified term catalog: expected term or provided term.

    See also

    • Expected term (conv. 274)

    • Provided term (conv. 274)

    • Verified term catalog (conv. 274)

    Term variant (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a different wording used for an existing term that shares its defining category.

    ~ is acceptable if it is introduced and then used consistently.

    See also

    • Broader substitute (conv. 274)

    • Misused expected term (conv. 274)

    • Redundant term (conv. 274)

    Text (conv. 274)#

    ~ is information structured by connecting terms for the purpose of encoding knowledge.

    See also

    • Term (conv. 274)

    Unabridged term register (conv. 274)#

    ~ is the record of everything retrieved from a text as a possible term: unverified terms, terms, and pseudo-terms.

    See also

    • Pseudo-term (conv. 274)

    • Unverified term (conv. 274)

    • Verified term catalog (conv. 274)

    Unqualified list (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a sequence that includes expected terms, provided terms, or both, that has no expected term introducing it. The sequence may mix expected and provided terms as long as its provided terms are not insider terms.

    Important

    Regardless of author’s privilege, a term that appears in more than one list requires every such list to be qualified.

    See also

    • Declared list (conv. 274)

    • Illustration (conv. 274)

    • Insider term (conv. 274)

    Unverified term (conv. 274)#

    ~ is a word, phrase, or statement found in text before it is confirmed to be an expected term, a provided term, or a pseudo-term.

    See also

    • Expected term (conv. 274)

    • Provided term (conv. 274)

    • Pseudo-term (conv. 274)

    Verified term catalog (conv. 274)#

    ~ is the record of the terms that have passed verification, each with its term association.

    See also

    • Derived term (conv. 274)

    • Statement (conv. 274)

    • Term association (conv. 274)

    • Unabridged term register (conv. 274)
